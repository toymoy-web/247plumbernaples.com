Repair Or Replace

When A Disposal Is Worth Fixing

A jam or a tripped reset switch is almost always worth repairing rather than replacing, since it's a quick, inexpensive fix. A leaking unit is a different story: once the internal seal fails, water finds its way past it consistently, and repair parts for that specific failure are rarely cost-effective compared to a new unit. We'll tell you honestly which category your disposal falls into rather than defaulting to replacement.

A Few Things Worth Knowing

What Actually Damages A Disposal

Fibrous foods like celery and corn husks, grease poured down the drain, and hard items like bones or fruit pits are the most common causes of jams and premature wear. None of these will necessarily break a disposal on the first try, but repeated exposure shortens the unit's life meaningfully. If your disposal jams often, it's worth a quick conversation about what's going down it before assuming the unit itself is simply bad, since a habit change costs nothing compared to another repair call.

Installation Details

What A New Install Involves

A disposal installation includes disconnecting and removing the old unit, mounting the new one to the existing sink flange when compatible, and reconnecting the drain and dishwasher lines. If the old mounting hardware is worn or the flange itself needs replacement, we'll flag that before starting rather than forcing a new unit onto old hardware that won't hold it properly.

Sizing A Replacement

Horsepower And Household Needs

Garbage disposals are rated by motor horsepower, generally from 1/3 HP for light residential use up to 1 HP for heavy daily use or larger households. A disposal that's undersized for how a kitchen actually gets used will jam more often and burn out sooner, while an oversized unit for a light-use kitchen is simply spending more than necessary. We size based on how your household actually cooks, not a default recommendation.

Septic System Note

Disposals And Septic Tanks Don't Always Mix Well

For homes on septic systems, common across Golden Gate Estates and Immokalee, a garbage disposal adds solid waste load to the tank that a household without one wouldn't have. This doesn't mean skipping a disposal entirely, but it does mean pumping the tank on a somewhat shorter interval than a household without one, and being more mindful about what goes down it in the first place. We'll factor your septic setup into any installation recommendation.

Kitchens We Service

Condo Kitchens In Park Shore, Family Homes In Lely

Disposals jam most often from fibrous and starchy food, and the fix is usually a reset or clearing the impeller. In condos along Gulf Shore Boulevard and in Park Shore, a disposal that keeps jamming is also a drain-line warning, because a slow shared stack backs up the sink first. The Pelican Bay and Vanderbilt Beach buildings are covered under North Naples.

In homes on septic, including parts of Golden Gate Estates, we recommend going easy on the disposal altogether, since extra solids fill a tank faster. Replacement and installation follow the manufacturer's specs and the Florida Building Code, Plumbing.

Common Questions

Disposal FAQ

My disposal is humming but won't spin. What's wrong?

Almost always a jam. Many units have a manual reset that can clear this, which we'll check first before recommending anything more involved.

How long does disposal installation take?

Most installations, including removal of the old unit, take under an hour.

Is a leaking disposal a plumbing emergency?

Not usually urgent in the way a burst pipe is, but worth addressing promptly since continued leaking can damage the cabinet below the sink.

What size disposal do I actually need?

It depends on your household's cooking habits and how often the kitchen sees heavy use. We can recommend the right horsepower once we know how the kitchen is actually used.

Is it okay to have a disposal with a septic system?

Yes, but it means somewhat more frequent septic pumping and being mindful about what goes down it. We can advise on both when we install, and coordinate with your regular septic service schedule if you already have one.

Can I run hot water while using the disposal?

Cold water is actually preferred while running a disposal, since it helps solidify any grease so it grinds and clears rather than coating the pipe walls further down the line. Running hot water can actually melt grease and let it pass through only to re-solidify further along the line.
