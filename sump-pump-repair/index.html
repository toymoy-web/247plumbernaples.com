An Honest Starting Point

Does A Naples Home Even Need A Sump Pump?

Often, no. Most Naples homes are slab-on-grade with no basement, so the deep sump pits common in northern basements are rare here. The ground is also close to the water table in much of Florida, which is why pumps appear in specific places and not everywhere.

Where we do see them: garages and stairwells set below grade, lower-level rooms in multi-level homes, drainage pits tied to French drains on low lots, and sewage ejector basins for a bathroom or wet bar that sits below the main sewer line. Each one fails differently, so the first job is working out which kind of pump you have.

Pump Types

Three Machines That All Get Called A Sump Pump

  • Sump pumps sit in a pit and remove groundwater and rain. The discharge line must run outdoors and away from the foundation, never into a septic system or the sewer.
  • Drainage pumps serve French drains and low spots in a yard or garage, and they usually run on a float switch the same way a sump pump does.
  • Sewage ejector pumps lift wastewater and solids from fixtures that sit below the sewer line. They live in a sealed, vented basin, and a failure there is a sanitary problem and not just a wet floor.
What Fails

Why Pumps Quit, In The Order We Usually Find It

⚙️

Stuck Float Switch

The pump never turns on, or never turns off. A stuck float is the most common failure and often the cheapest fix.

🔄

Failed Check Valve

Water drains back into the pit after each cycle, so the pump short-cycles and wears out early.

🚫

Clogged Intake Or Discharge

Silt and debris block the intake, or a crushed or frozen-open discharge line sends water back where it started.

🔥

Burned-Out Motor

A pump that runs all wet season against a high water table eventually wears out, and replacement is the honest answer.

Power Outages

Storms Take Out The Power And The Pump Together

The same storm that fills the pit often cuts the power that runs the pump. A battery backup can keep a primary pump going for a limited time, typically a few hours up to about a day, depending on the battery and how fast the pit refills.

We size the backup to the pit and the outages you are likely to see, and we test it on the visit. If the pit keeps filling past what a battery can handle, the real fix is usually better drainage, and we tell you that plainly. Water that reaches the floor is a job for our water damage restoration team.

Where We Repair Pumps

Low Lots, Canal Streets, And Garages Below Grade

Low-lying areas such as East Naples and Golden Gate Estates are where pump and drainage problems come up most after heavy rain. On Naples Bay and the canal streets of Royal Harbor and Aqualane Shores, the ground sits close to the water, so any below-grade pit there works against a high water table.

Beach-side buildings in Pelican Bay, Park Shore, and Vanderbilt Beach can have pump-assisted drains for garage levels and lower-level rooms. Pump and discharge work follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and we confirm whether a permit applies before we start. If water keeps showing up and you are not sure whether it is groundwater or a supply line, our leak detection can tell the two apart.

What To Expect

From First Call To A Working Pump

We start by asking what kind of pump you have, where the discharge goes, and whether the pit is full right now. If it is, do not stand in water to check the pump. Turn off its breaker first, keep cords and outlets clear of the water, and call us.

On site we test the float, the check valve, the discharge line, and the electrical supply, then tell you what failed and what it costs before any work starts. Most repairs finish in a single visit, and a replacement pump is usually installed the same day.

Common Questions

Sump Pump Repair FAQ

Do I need a sump pump in Naples?

Often not. Most Naples homes are slab-on-grade with no basement, so deep sump pits are rare here. Pumps show up where a garage, stairwell, or lower level sits below grade, where a French drain needs somewhere to send water, or where a bathroom sits below the sewer line.

Can a sump pump discharge into my septic tank or the sewer?

No. Sump water goes outdoors and away from the foundation. Sending it into a septic system can overload the drain field, and sending it into the sewer line is not allowed.

What is the difference between a sump pump and a sewage ejector pump?

A sump pump moves groundwater and rain out of a pit. A sewage ejector pump lifts wastewater and solids from fixtures that sit below the sewer line, in a sealed and vented basin. They look similar and fail in different ways.

How long does a battery backup last?

Typically a few hours up to about a day, depending on the battery and how fast the pit refills. We size the backup to your pit and the outages you are likely to see, then test it before we leave.

My pump runs constantly. Is it broken?

Not always. A high water table or a stretch of heavy rain keeps a pump running, and a failed check valve makes it cycle again and again. We check both before we recommend replacing anything.

Is a failed sump pump an emergency?

If the pit is full and rain or a storm is on the way, yes. We treat that as a same-day call, and our emergency line runs 24/7.
