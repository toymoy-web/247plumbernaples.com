Repiping Historic Old Naples Homes

Old Naples is home to some of Collier County's oldest housing stock, and repiping one of these homes takes a different approach than a standard modern repipe.

Original Materials

What's Actually Running Through These Walls

Homes built in Old Naples decades ago were plumbed with whatever was standard at the time, often galvanized steel or early copper, both of which have well-documented aging patterns by now. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, gradually restricting flow and eventually failing, often without much external warning until a leak or a dramatic pressure drop shows up. Copper from that same era, while more durable, isn't immune to pinhole leaks after enough decades in service.

Knowing which material a specific home has, rather than assuming based on the build year alone, matters, since some Old Naples homes have had partial updates over the years that leave a mix of old and newer pipe in different parts of the house.

Working Around Character

Repiping Without Tearing The Home Apart

A historic home's finishes, original hardwood, plaster walls, period trim, are usually a big part of what makes the property valuable and worth preserving. We plan repipe routing to minimize disruption to those finishes wherever possible, sometimes routing through less visible spaces like closets, attics, or existing chases rather than cutting through a feature wall. It takes more planning upfront than a standard repipe in a newer home, but it's worth doing right.

Permitting Considerations

Historic District Rules Worth Knowing About

Depending on the specific property and whether it falls under any historic designation or overlay district, exterior work or visible changes may require additional review beyond a standard plumbing permit. Interior repiping work generally doesn't trigger the same scrutiny as exterior changes, but it's worth confirming your property's specific status before any work begins, something we're happy to help sort out as part of the planning process.

Signs It's Time

How To Know A Historic Home Actually Needs A Repipe

Recurring pinhole leaks in different spots over time, noticeably low water pressure that's worsened gradually, and discolored water when a faucet is first turned on are all signs of galvanized or aging copper pipe reaching the end of its usable life. A single leak doesn't necessarily mean the whole system needs replacing, but a pattern of them across different fixtures usually does.

Material Choices

What We Typically Repipe Into

PEX piping has become the standard choice for most residential repipes, including in historic homes, thanks to its flexibility, which makes routing through tight or awkward spaces significantly easier than rigid copper, and its strong track record on longevity. Copper remains an option where a homeowner prefers it, particularly for exposed runs where appearance matters, and we'll walk through the tradeoffs for your specific home rather than defaulting to one material across the board.

Planning The Project

What A Historic Home Repipe Actually Involves

We start with a full assessment of the existing plumbing, mapping out what material is running where and identifying which routes will minimize disruption to original finishes. From there, we plan the sequence of work room by room, coordinating around anything that needs special protection, exposed hardwood, plaster walls, original tile, so those finishes come through the project intact.

Homeowners are kept informed at each stage rather than discovering mid-project that a routing decision affects a space they cared about preserving.

A Long-Term Investment

Why This Work Pays Off Over Time

A properly executed repipe on a historic home addresses the underlying plumbing issue once, comprehensively, rather than through a series of patch repairs on failing galvanized or aging copper that each buy a little more time before the next problem surfaces.

For a home you plan to keep in the family or that represents a genuine long-term investment, that comprehensive approach tends to be the more sensible one financially, even with the higher upfront cost compared to another round of spot repairs.

Insurance And Inspections

What Buyers And Insurers Look For

Aging galvanized or original copper piping is a flag that comes up regularly during a home inspection ahead of a sale, and some insurers scrutinize older plumbing systems more closely when underwriting or renewing a policy on a historic property. A documented repipe with current permits on file addresses both concerns directly, which is worth factoring in even if the plumbing itself hasn't caused a problem yet.

City Historic Rules

What The City Of Naples Says About Historic Buildings

The City of Naples has a historic building ordinance, and its Building page says work on a historic building must be reviewed by the Florida State Historic Preservation Office before it is submitted to the city for a building permit. A building counts as historic if it is individually listed in the National Register, contributes to a listed district, or has been found eligible by the State Historic Preservation Officer.

The page also lists minor plumbing, electrical, and mechanical work that does not alter historic design, materials, or significance as exempt from that review. Because the rule depends on whether your home is a historic building and what the work touches, ask the City's Building Department before you open any walls.

EPA notes lead service lines are most often found in homes built before 1986, so ask your utility about the line at the street. We serve homes in Old Naples, East Naples, and North Naples.

Safety Checklist

  • Use cold tap water for drinking and cooking, as the EPA advises where lead is a concern.
  • Do not disturb old pipe or solder yourself. Call a plumber and document the work for your insurance records.
  • Avoid hot water for baby formula or cooking until the plumbing has been assessed.

Sources and guidelines: City of Naples historic building ordinance page, U.S. EPA lead in drinking water pages.

Common Questions

Historic Home Repiping FAQ

How long does a full repipe take on a historic home?

It varies by home size and routing complexity, but a typical repipe runs a few days for most single-family homes, with careful scheduling around any finishes that need extra protection.

Will I need to move out during the repipe?

Most homeowners stay in the home during the work, though water service is interrupted at points during the process. We coordinate timing so disruption stays minimal.

Does repiping increase a historic home's resale value?

Updated plumbing is a genuine selling point for older homes, since buyers and inspectors alike flag aging galvanized or copper pipe as a concern during a purchase.

Can you match new fixtures to a historic home's original style?

Yes, we can source fixtures that fit a period-appropriate aesthetic where that matters to you, and we're happy to coordinate on style choices alongside the functional repipe work itself, whether that means classic finishes or a more modern look layered into the historic shell. We can also work from photos or examples you've collected to help guide fixture selection throughout the home.

Does repiping an Old Naples historic home need state review?

The City of Naples says work on a historic building must be reviewed by the Florida State Historic Preservation Office before the city building permit submittal, while minor plumbing that does not alter historic design is listed as exempt. Ask the City's Building Department whether your home counts as historic.
