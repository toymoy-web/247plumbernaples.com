Not Sure What You Need?

Filtration Solves A Different Problem Than Softening

Filtration removes chlorine, sediment, and other contaminants that affect taste, smell, and water clarity. Softening specifically addresses hardness minerals that cause scale buildup. Many households genuinely need both, and we test your actual water before recommending one, the other, or a combined system rather than assuming.

Signs You Need Filtration

What Naples Homeowners Notice

🧪

Chlorine Taste Or Smell

Common on municipal water, treatable with carbon-based whole-house filtration.

🏖️

Visible Sediment

Sandy or cloudy water is more common on private wells across Golden Gate Estates and Immokalee.

🦠

Sulfur Or "Rotten Egg" Smell

Usually hydrogen sulfide, common in certain well water, treatable with the right filtration media.

🟤

Rust-Colored Staining

Iron content, common on some wells, requiring iron-specific filtration rather than a standard carbon filter.

Testing First

Why We Test Before Recommending Equipment

Naples' municipal water and the private wells across Collier County's unincorporated areas have genuinely different water chemistry, and even two wells on neighboring properties can differ meaningfully in mineral and sediment content. We test your actual water, not a general assumption about your area, before recommending a specific filtration setup, so the system you get is sized and configured for what's really in your water.

Related Services

Filtration And Softening Together

A combined filtration and water softener setup is common for well-water households dealing with both hardness and sediment or odor issues. Clean, properly filtered water ahead of a tankless water heater or standard tank also helps protect that investment from scale and sediment buildup over time.

Ongoing Maintenance

What Keeps A System Working

Whole-house filtration systems need periodic filter or media replacement to keep performing, typically every three to twelve months depending on the filter type and how much water the household uses. A system running on exhausted media stops filtering effectively without necessarily showing an obvious sign, which is why we set up a realistic replacement schedule at installation rather than leaving it to guesswork.

Additional Options

UV Treatment And Reverse Osmosis

For households on private wells with bacterial concerns, a UV treatment stage can be added to a whole-house system to neutralize pathogens without chemicals. Reverse osmosis, typically installed at a single point like the kitchen tap rather than whole-house, provides the highest level of purification for drinking water specifically. These are add-ons worth discussing if your water test reveals a specific concern that standard carbon filtration doesn't fully address, rather than something we add by default to every installation.

Installation Process

What Installation Day Looks Like

Installation typically happens at the point where the main water line enters the home, ahead of the water heater so both hot and cold water throughout the house benefit from filtration. Most whole-house systems install in half a day to a full day depending on complexity and whether existing plumbing needs modification to accommodate the new unit.

We test the water again after installation to confirm the system is actually performing as expected, not just assume it is because it's connected.

Filtration By Neighborhood

Taste, Chlorine, And Wells From Pelican Bay To The Estates

On city water in Pelican Bay, Park Shore, and Old Naples, filtration is mostly about taste, chlorine, and sediment, and a carbon system is usually enough. On private wells in Golden Gate Estates and outlying Immokalee, the questions are iron, hardness, and bacteria, and the system starts with a lab test.

In Bonita Springs, the utility treats a blend of lime-softened and reverse-osmosis water, so the right system often looks different from the one in Naples. We would rather tell you that than sell a system your water does not need.

Common Questions

Water Filtration FAQ

Do I need filtration if I'm already on city water?

Many households on municipal water still want filtration to address chlorine taste and smell, even though the water is already treated and safe.

Can one system handle both filtration and softening?

Yes, combination systems exist and are common for well-water households dealing with multiple issues at once.

How do I know what's actually in my water?

We test it. Guessing based on symptoms alone can lead to the wrong system, so testing comes before any recommendation.

Will filtration lower my water pressure?

A properly sized system shouldn't cause a noticeable pressure drop. An undersized filter is the most common cause of reduced pressure after installation, which is part of why correct sizing matters.

Is a whole-house system better than a countertop filter?

For anything beyond drinking water, yes. A countertop or pitcher filter only treats the water you pour through it, while a whole-house system treats everything, including shower and bath water.
