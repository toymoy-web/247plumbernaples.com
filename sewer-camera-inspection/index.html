If The Camera Finds A Problem

If the inspection shows a crack, offset joint, or significant root intrusion, that becomes a sewer line repair conversation with real footage to back up the recommendation, not a guess. If it's just buildup without structural damage, hydro jetting may be all that's needed.

What We're Actually Looking For

Reading The Footage

A camera inspection reveals several distinct issues, each with a different fix. Root intrusion looks like hair-like growth breaking through a joint. A belly, a low spot where the pipe has settled, shows as standing water pooling on camera even when nothing is actively draining.

An offset joint appears as a visible step where two pipe sections no longer line up cleanly. Cracks and breaks are usually obvious once the camera reaches them. We narrate what we're seeing in real time rather than reviewing the footage separately afterward, so you understand the finding as it happens.

Naples-Specific Note

Why This Matters More In Older Neighborhoods

Homes in Naples' established neighborhoods, particularly those built before the 1980s, are more likely to have clay or cast iron laterals with no inspection history at all. A camera inspection is often the first time these lines have ever actually been looked at since installation, which is exactly why repeat clogs in an older home deserve this step rather than another round of snaking that treats the symptom without confirming the cause.

For Buyers And Sellers

A Real-Estate Transaction Use Case

A general home inspection typically doesn't include the sewer lateral, since it requires specialized camera equipment most home inspectors don't carry. For a home built before the 1980s, or any home with unknown sewer history, a camera inspection ahead of closing can surface a costly repair before it becomes the new owner's surprise, or give a seller documentation to address concerns before they derail a sale.

The Equipment We Use

What Makes A Reliable Inspection

A waterproof camera head on a flexible push cable enters through an accessible cleanout and travels the length of the line, sending live video back to a monitor on the truck.

A locator built into the equipment lets us mark the exact above-ground position and depth of anything significant the camera finds, useful information if a spot repair ends up being the next step.

This is meaningfully different from a basic handheld camera, which lacks the reach and locating capability to inspect a full residential lateral, and it's why a genuine inspection takes real equipment rather than a quick look with a flashlight.

Where Camera Inspections Pay Off

Before You Buy Near Naples Bay Or The Beach

A camera inspection is most useful before a purchase, and Naples has a lot of older waterfront housing where that matters, from Royal Harbor on the eastern shore of Naples Bay to the canal streets of Aqualane Shores and The Moorings. Across East Naples, the same inspection helps on older homes near US 41.

It is just as useful when a line keeps clogging in places like Naples Park or Naples Manor. A recording of the pipe shows roots, offsets, and low spots, so the repair is quoted from what is actually there. We can give you the footage for the seller, the association, or your insurer.

Find The Source

A Camera Visit To Locate A Suspected Leak Or Smell

People schedule a camera inspection for reasons that go beyond a purchase. A slow drain that smells of sewer, a P-trap that will not hold water, or a leak someone suspects in the sewer line are all good reasons to look inside the pipe instead of guessing.

The camera also tells us whether a leak is from the drain line or the supply line, which saves a lot of wasted digging. We can mark the depth and position on the surface so repairs, if needed, are targeted.

Common Questions

Camera Inspection FAQ

Do I get a copy of the video?

Yes, we provide the recording so you have it for your own records or to share with an insurer, buyer, or seller.

Can this be done before I buy a home?

Yes, this is a common pre-purchase check, especially for homes over 20 years old where the sewer line's condition and material aren't documented.

How long does an inspection take?

Most residential inspections take 30 to 60 minutes depending on the length and layout of the line.

What if the camera can't get through the whole line?

That itself is a finding, usually meaning a significant blockage or collapse at that point, and we'll explain what we found before recommending next steps, including whether the blockage itself needs to be cleared before a full inspection can continue.

Can the camera find a leak in a sewer line?

It can show cracks, offsets, and separated joints, and we mark where they are on the surface. Pressurized supply leaks are found with leak detection equipment, which is a different tool.
