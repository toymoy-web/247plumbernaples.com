What To Do Immediately

  • Find your main water shutoff valve, usually near the street-side meter or where the line enters the garage, and turn it off.
  • Turn off electricity to any affected area if water is near outlets or fixtures, if you can do so safely.
  • Move furniture and valuables away from the water if it's safe to do so.
  • Call us. Once the main is off, the immediate flooding stops, but the pipe still needs repair before you can safely turn water back on.
Why This Happens

Common Causes In Naples Homes

🧊

Sudden Pressure Spike

A water hammer event or a failed pressure regulator can rupture an already-weakened section of pipe.

🦠

Corrosion

Older galvanized or copper pipe corroded from the inside eventually fails at its weakest point.

🏗️

Physical Damage

Landscaping work, a settling foundation, or accidental impact can crack a line.

Related Services

If There's Already Water Damage

Stopping the pipe and drying out the property are two different jobs. If water has already reached drywall, flooring, or belongings, our water damage restoration team can be dispatched alongside the pipe repair so both happen close together. If the same pipe material has burst more than once, it's worth a conversation about whole-home repiping instead of repeated emergency repairs.

Installed To Code

Repairs That Hold Up

Every burst pipe repair follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, the statewide standard licensed plumbers work under for pipe material and pressure testing, and gets pressure tested before we consider the job done. An emergency repair is still a permanent repair in our approach, not a stopgap that needs revisiting in a few months. If the failure points to a broader issue with your home's piping, we'll tell you honestly rather than just patching the one spot and moving on.

Why It Happened

Understanding The Cause Prevents The Next One

A burst pipe rarely happens for no reason. Age and corrosion, a sudden pressure spike, or physical damage from nearby work are the three most common causes, and each points to a different follow-up. We'll explain what we find during the repair, not just fix the immediate break, so you know whether this was an isolated event or a sign of something worth addressing across the rest of the home's plumbing.

Storm Season Note

Power Outages Raise The Risk

During hurricane season, an extended power outage can affect well pumps and pressure systems in ways that increase stress on pipes when power returns, and storm winds can physically damage exposed exterior lines. If a burst happens during or right after a storm, getting to your main shutoff and calling promptly matters even more, since restoration crews and plumbers are both in higher demand during active weather events, and early calls get dispatched faster.

Common Questions

Burst Pipe FAQ

I can't find my main shutoff valve. What do I do?

Call us immediately. We can walk you through locating it over the phone in many cases, based on your home's age and layout, or dispatch as quickly as possible if you can't find it.

Should I try to repair a small leak myself before you arrive?

We'd rather you focus on shutting off the water and staying safe than attempting a repair. A temporary fix can sometimes make the actual repair harder or mask the real extent of the damage.

How fast can you get here?

Burst pipes are treated as priority calls on our 24/7 line, dispatched same-hour whenever possible.

Can I turn my water back on myself after the leak stops?

Not until the pipe is actually repaired. Turning water back on to a broken line will cause the same flooding again.

Is a burst pipe covered by homeowners insurance?

Most policies cover sudden, accidental damage from a burst pipe, though coverage for the repair itself versus resulting water damage can vary. Check your specific policy or ask your insurer, and keep photos of the damage for your claim.

How much water can a burst pipe actually release?

A fully burst supply line can release well over a hundred gallons per hour, which is why shutting off the main immediately is the single most important thing you can do before we arrive. A quarter-inch supply line break can flood a room within an hour if left unaddressed.
