Install Standards

Done To Code, Not Just Done

Every installation follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, including required expansion tanks, proper venting for gas units, and code-compliant drain pan placement. Skipping these on a replacement is a common shortcut that shows up later as a failed inspection at resale or, worse, an actual safety issue. We install to code the first time.

Sizing It Right

Getting The Capacity Right The First Time

An undersized tank means running out of hot water during normal household use, while an oversized one wastes energy keeping water hot that never gets used.

We size the replacement to your household's actual fixture count and peak usage, not just a like-for-like swap of whatever was there before, since a home's hot water demand can change over the years a previous unit was installed.

A household that's added a bathroom or grown in size since the original installation often needs a genuinely larger unit, not the same capacity replaced automatically.

Naples-Specific Note

Garage Installations And Flood Elevation

Many Naples homes keep the water heater in the garage, and in flood-prone areas of Collier County that can mean an elevation requirement for the unit above the garage floor. We check this as part of every replacement so the new installation meets current code, not just matches what was there before, which may have predated the current requirement.

Electric Vs. Gas Vs. Heat Pump

Choosing A Fuel Type

Electric tank units are the simplest to install and the most common default when no gas line already runs to the water heater location. Gas units heat faster and typically cost less to run where natural gas or propane is already available.

Heat pump water heaters use ambient air to move heat rather than generating it directly, which makes them notably efficient in Naples' warm climate, though they need adequate air space around the unit to work well and cost more upfront. We'll walk through what your home already has and what switching would actually involve before you decide.

Replacement Jobs By Neighborhood

Permits, Condo Rules, And Tight Closets

Replacing a water heater is permitted work, and in Pelican Bay, Park Shore, and Vanderbilt Beach the building association often adds its own rules on top, from approved delivery hours to insurance paperwork. We confirm those requirements before the old tank comes out so the swap does not stall. The Pelican Bay and Vanderbilt Beach buildings are covered under North Naples.

In older single-family areas like Naples Park, Old Naples, and Royal Harbor, access is usually easier but code updates can be harder, since venting, pan drains, and expansion tanks may need to be brought up to current standards. Tell us the neighborhood on the first call and we will plan the day around it.

Failed Tank

Emergency Replacement When A Tank Fails

Some tanks give warning, and others split open overnight. A tank that has rusted through cannot be repaired, and the quickest way back to hot water is a replacement installed the same day or the next, depending on the model you want.

When you call our 24/7 line, tell us the fuel type, the approximate size, and where the heater sits. Those three details let us bring the right unit and connections instead of making a second trip.

A flooded garage or closet should be dealt with the same day. Close the cold water supply and shut off power or gas first, then call.

Changing Heater Type

Switching From Gas To A Heat Pump Or Tankless Heater

Some customers want to swap a gas tank for an electric heat pump model, or a tank for a tankless unit. The U.S. Department of Energy says heat pump water heaters can be two to three times more energy efficient than conventional electric resistance heaters, and that they need space that stays roughly between 40 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit year round.

A change of type matters for permitting. Collier County's permit page says a change of type, such as electric to gas or tank to tankless, goes through standard review and calls for plumbing plans, instead of the self-issued permit used for like-for-like swaps. Ask about timing when you plan a switch, and note that emergency replacements are usually like for like.

Common Questions

Replacement FAQ

Can you replace my water heater the same day I call?

In most cases, yes, assuming the unit is in stock for your home's configuration. We'll confirm timing when you call.

Do you haul away the old unit?

Yes, removal and disposal of the old water heater is included with a replacement.

Is switching from a tank to tankless a same-day job?

Usually not, since it often requires new venting or gas line work. We'll give you an accurate timeline once we see your setup.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Naples?

Most jurisdictions in Collier County require a permit for water heater replacement. We handle the permitting as part of the job.

Is a heat pump water heater worth the extra cost in Florida?

For many households, yes, since Naples' warm climate is close to ideal for how these units work. The payback period depends on your household's hot water usage and current energy costs, which we can walk through for your specific situation.

How fast can a failed water heater be replaced?

For an emergency replacement, often the same day or the next, depending on the unit you choose and whether a like-for-like model is available. Call our 24/7 line and tell us the fuel type, size, and location.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Collier County?

The county treats water heater replacement as a permit type, and a change of type, size, or location goes through standard review. Check with the Collier County Building Division if you are unsure, and permit requirements are checked before the install.

What qualifies as an emergency water heater replacement?

A tank that is leaking heavily, flooding a garage or closet, or has failed completely with no hot water for the household. Shut off the cold supply and power, then call our 24/7 line.
