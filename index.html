Local Know-How

Why Naples Plumbing Is Different

Three things about this area change how a plumbing problem actually shows up, and what fixing it correctly looks like.

🏝️

Slab Foundations

Most Naples homes are built on a concrete slab rather than a basement or crawlspace, so a supply-line leak often surfaces as a warm spot on the floor or a spike in the water bill long before it is visible.

🌀

Hurricane Season

Naples sits directly on the Gulf, and wind-driven rain during storm season can push water into homes through roof and slab penetrations, which is why we treat water damage calls as time-sensitive year-round.

🚰

Well & Septic Areas

Golden Gate Estates, Immokalee, and Ave Maria sit outside Naples' municipal sewer and water service, so private wells and septic tanks are the norm rather than the exception in those areas.

Emergency Plumbing Dispatch: What Happens When You Call

People call our emergency line for very different reasons. A refrigerator water line leaking across the kitchen floor, a water heater flooding a garage, a leak inside a bathroom wall, a toilet that gurgles and will not drain, or a main line that is backing up. All of those are worth a phone call right now instead of a wait.

When you call, we ask where the water is, whether you have been able to shut it off, and what the home looks like. That is enough to decide who goes first and what goes on the truck. If you cannot find the shutoff, we talk you through finding it while we dispatch.

Common Questions

Naples Plumbing FAQ

Do you offer free estimates?

Yes, for non-emergency work. Emergency dispatch carries a stated call-out fee, disclosed before we head out, and every repair still gets a written price before we start.

Do you service well and septic homes in Golden Gate Estates and Immokalee?

Yes. A large share of our Collier County calls are from homes on private wells and septic tanks rather than city water and sewer.

What causes slab leaks in Naples homes?

Sandy, shifting soil under a concrete slab combined with older copper supply lines is the most common cause we see, particularly in homes built before the 1990s.

Are you available during hurricane season?

Yes, our emergency line runs 24/7 through storm season. Wind-driven rain intrusion and burst pipes from power loss are both treated as priority calls.

Do you handle commercial properties, including grease traps and property-management work orders?

Yes. We work directly with property managers on standard work orders in addition to residential service.

Why do Marco Island homes see more pipe corrosion?

Salt-laden air off the Gulf accelerates corrosion on exposed metal fittings and older galvanized supply lines faster than it does further inland, which is why we check exterior connections closely on coastal service calls.

What should I check before hurricane season?

Have any known slow leaks fixed beforehand, know where your main shutoff valve is, and if you use a sump pump, confirm it has a working battery backup in case of a power outage during the storm.

How fast can an emergency plumber get to my Naples home?

Arrival depends on call volume, your location in Collier County, and the problem. Tell us where you are and what is happening, and we give you a realistic arrival window on the call, with active leaks and backups going first.

While You Wait

What To Do Before We Arrive

A few steps in the first few minutes of a plumbing emergency can limit the damage before a plumber reaches your door.

  • Find and shut off the main water valve if a pipe has burst or a supply line is spraying. In most Naples homes this is near the street-side meter or where the line enters the garage.
  • For a water heater leak, shut off the cold water supply valve at the top of the tank and switch off the power breaker or gas supply before you leave the area.
  • Move furniture, electronics, and rugs away from standing water and photograph the affected area for your insurance file before cleanup begins.
  • Do not run a disposal or flush a toilet if a drain is backing up. Continuing to run water pushes contaminated water further into the home.
  • If you smell gas near a water heater or gas line, leave the house, do not operate light switches, and call your gas utility's emergency line in addition to us.
