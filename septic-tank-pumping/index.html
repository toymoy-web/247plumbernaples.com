Why Septic Is The Norm Here

Well & Septic In Collier County's Unincorporated Areas

Unlike Naples proper, much of Golden Gate Estates is made up of one-acre-plus lots on private wells and septic systems rather than municipal water and sewer. The same is true across most of Immokalee and large parts of Ave Maria. A septic system that isn't pumped on schedule, typically every three to five years for an average household, is the most common cause of the slow drains and yard odors we get called out for in these areas.

Signs You Need Service

When A Septic Tank Needs Attention

🌀

Slow Drains Throughout The House

When every drain in the house is slow at once, and there's no visible clog, a full tank is a common cause on septic systems.

🦠

Sewage Odor In The Yard

A persistent smell near the drain field usually means the tank needs pumping or the field needs inspection.

🌱

Unusually Lush Grass Over The Field

A patch of grass that's noticeably greener than the rest of the yard can mean effluent is surfacing.

🚽

Gurgling Toilets

Toilets that gurgle when other fixtures drain often point to a septic system reaching capacity.

Related Services

Well & Septic Go Together

Most homes on a septic system in Golden Gate Estates and Immokalee are also on a private well rather than city water. If your water pressure has dropped or the well pump is cycling more than usual, ask about well pump repair during the same visit. And if hard well water is leaving scale on fixtures or shortening your water heater's life, a water softener is worth discussing too.

Septic systems in Florida are regulated under Chapter 64E-6 of the Florida Administrative Code, the Florida Department of Health's standard for onsite sewage treatment and disposal systems. That code sets the requirements for tank sizing, drain field design, and system maintenance that a properly installed septic system in Collier County has to meet, which is part of why a full system inspection sometimes surfaces issues beyond a simple pump-out.

What We Check

A Pumping Visit Is Also An Inspection

Every pumping visit includes a look at the tank's condition, the baffles, and, where accessible, an early read on drain field performance. Catching a cracked baffle or a drain field that's starting to struggle during a routine pump-out is far less expensive than waiting for a full system failure. If we see something that needs more than pumping, we'll explain exactly what and why before recommending it, not fold it into a bigger invoice without a conversation first.

If It's Already Backing Up

What To Do Before We Arrive

  • Stop running water in the house, including laundry and dishwashing, until the system is pumped.
  • Keep people and pets away from any area where effluent has surfaced in the yard.
  • Avoid flushing toilets more than necessary until service arrives.
  • Note which fixtures back up first, that detail helps us diagnose faster on arrival.
Common Questions

Septic Service FAQ

How often should a septic tank be pumped?

Every three to five years for an average household, depending on tank size and how many people live in the home.

Do you service septic systems in Immokalee and Ave Maria, not just Golden Gate Estates?

Yes, we cover all three areas along with the rest of unincorporated Collier County.

Can you find my septic tank if I don't know where it is?

Yes, we can locate the tank as part of the service call if you don't have records showing its location.

What should I avoid putting down the drain on a septic system?

Grease, wipes labeled flushable, and harsh chemical drain cleaners all shorten a septic system's life. Grease coats the tank and lines, wipes don't break down and can clog the outlet, and harsh chemicals can kill the bacteria that break down solids inside the tank.
